Research notes

1 · The metric, re-derived

Transcribed line by line from the problem page and verified against a brute-force O(N·|G|) implementation to 1e-9 (tests/test_metric.py). FNw = |G| − TPw exactly, which collapses the score to DTI = T / (0.2T + 0.2(S − M) + 0.8|G|) and gives the marginal-credit rule k > 0.2·DTI.

3 · The size of the hidden truth

The 0.2778 raster was produced from the 0.2708 raster by deleting the 2,545 dots within 200 m of the catalogue. Assuming those dots earned no credit (they sit on faults the organiser does not score), the two reported scores and the two masses give two equations in T and |G|:

0.2708 = T / (0.2·40199 + 0.8·|G|)
0.2778 = T / (0.2·37654 + 0.8·|G|)   ⇒   |G| ≈ 15,200 px,  T ≈ 5,470

So the family's best raster captures roughly 36 % of the weighted truth. At the same 37,654-dot budget a perfect field would score |G| / (0.2·S + 0.8·|G|) ≈ 0.77. The distance between 0.28 and 0.77 is detector quality, and it is the entire prize.

4 · Chance level

Measured, not assumed: 0.03973 for 37,654 random dots at the same geometry. Without that number a holdout DTI of 0.04 is uninterpretable, because the metric pays any sparse dot set.

5 · What the geological literature says about where the new faults are

  • [official] The organiser's hidden set is "faults ... not contained within the current public USGS database", manually labelled by experts.
  • [literature] The INGENIOUS mappers "utilized LiDAR and hillshades ... to identify the extent and location of Quaternary faults", using "one-meter LiDAR from the GeoDAWN project ... provided by the USGS" (Giddens et al., Stanford Geothermal Workshop 2025). This is the direct justification for the lidar lane.
  • [literature] The same work reports gravity as "the most useful geophysical dataset in defining the extent and geometries of the major Quaternary faults" and step-overs as the most common favourable structural setting (~63 %, dominantly N–NNE striking).
  • [literature] Sare & Hilley, JOSS 2018 — scarplet template matching returns amplitude, relative age, orientation and SNR from a DEM. Steger, IEEE TPAMI 20(2):113–125, 1998 — sub-pixel line extraction from the Hessian eigenvector. Frangi et al., MICCAI 1998, LNCS 1496:130–137 — multiscale vesselness.
Deviation, stated not hidden. The 1 m tiles are not downloadable from this sandbox (IR-66-DATA-01), so the scarplet template matcher could not be re-run on native data. The lane is executed on the hash-pinned 1 m-derived per-100 m descriptor stack and on the 100 m detrended elevation, at 2–20 pixel (200–2000 m) scales.
