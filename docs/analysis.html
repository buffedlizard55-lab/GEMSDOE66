2. The Mathematical Mechanics: Why H33-2-B2 Achieved 0.2778

The competition evaluates predictions against a hidden truth set using the Distance-weighted Tversky Index (DTI) with α=0.2, β=0.8, and a 300 m triangular kernel:

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 · FP_w + 0.8 · FN_w)

where for each pixel within 300 m (3 pixels) of truth, credit decays linearly as K(d) = max(0, 1 - d/300). Crucial structural insights:

  • Asymmetric Penalty Ratio: False negatives are penalized at β=0.8 while false positives are penalized at α=0.2. A false negative is penalized 4 times heavier (β/α = 4.0) than a false positive.
  • Ground Truth Asymmetry: The evaluation target is new, previously unmapped faults discovered in the GeoDAWN region. The existing mapped catalogue faults (USGS / INGENIOUS) are excluded from the discovery truth set.
  • Catalogue-Flank Pruning Ladder: Across project history, emissions followed a monotonic ladder:
    • Dense baseline: 121,131 dots → 0.1922
    • Coarse thinning: 60,069 dots → 0.2477
    • d=2.8 px NMS thinning: 44,090 dots → 0.2600
    • Catalogue buffer B=1 (100 m): 40,199 dots → 0.2708
    • Catalogue buffer B=2 (200 m): 37,654 dots → 0.2778
  • Why B=2 is the Optimal Sweet Spot: Dots placed within 200 m of the known catalogue carry almost zero probability of being unmapped faults. Removing 2,545 dots reduces false positive mass (ΔFP_w ≈ -2545), reducing the denominator by 0.2 × 2545 ≈ 509 units without reducing true positive credit (dTP_w = 0). This directly inflates DTI: 
    ΔDTI ≈ TP_w / (D - 0.2 · dn) - TP_w / D > 0
  • Why B=3 Fails: At B=3 (300 m), genuine unmapped tip-line extensions, synthetic splays, and relay step-overs branch off the master fault. Pruning at 300 m removes true positives (dTP_w > 0). Because losing a true positive pixel costs -1.0 in the numerator and adds +0.8 in the denominator, the safety factor drops from 2.08 to 1.27, degrading live DTI.
