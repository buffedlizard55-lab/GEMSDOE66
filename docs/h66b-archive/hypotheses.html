Pre-implementation ranking

Four hypotheses, one lane

No code or score is attached to these proposals

Pre-implementation qualitative ranking by physical specificity, likely separation from named mimics, and cost. Sign and magnitude of DTI change are unmeasured; no score projection is made. Novelty against the full family registry is not established.

Only the lead proposal should enter implementation after scientific review and input/registry/tooling gates pass.

Shared preregistered protocol

  • Scale and output: [2, 20] m native features, aggregated to 100 m.
  • Holdout: Withhold whole vector-defined fault segments, not artificial raster chunks, from lidar-covered spatial blocks. Use a preregistered spatial buffer around held segments; recommend matching the 300 m scoring support, subject to review by the two independent scientific leads. Catalogue-derived features use visible faults only; visible fault pixels are masked exactly. Restrict truth and scoring to lidar-covered held-out blocks.
  • Metric: Pooled distance-weighted Tversky, alpha=0.2, beta=0.8, 300 m triangular kernel. Report evaluator version, withheld-positive count, and a 95% spatial-block CI for every HOLDOUT-DTI result.
  • Leakage: Test every feature alone on the spatially blocked holdout. AUC >0.90 is leakage until disproven. No hidden/withheld catalogue data may affect features, masks, candidate eligibility, or thresholds.
  • Range fronts: Compare gains in lidar-covered held-out blocks both overall and off a prespecified range-front stratum; reject a gain confined to range fronts.
  • Uniqueness: Compare continuous pre-placement surfaces and final dots against the complete registry. Correlation >0.90 or >70% of candidate dots within 3 px is a duplicate/lane-drift stop. Incomplete registry means BLOCKED, not PASS.
  • Budget: Maximum three experiments or two hours. No competition slot before the candidate strictly beats the current valid holdout best under the same protocol.
  • Inputs: The competition data page requires login in this session and the checkout has no official rasters or shared cached stack. USGS 3DEP provides public-domain 1 m bare-earth DEMs in principle, but exact competition-area tile coverage/alignment is not verified.
Rank 1 · LIDAR-01

Slope-aware scarp templates plus scale-persistent Steger/Frangi line evidence

Layers/features: Authorized 1 m bare-earth DEM tiles and the shared cached stack; Scarplet-style template outputs (best-fit amplitude, morphologic age, orientation, and template SNR); signed Steger sub-pixel ridge/valley responses and Frangi Hessian-line measures over 2–20 m scales; the existing cultural-feature mask only after its provenance, alignment, and availability are verified. Aggregate physical channel summaries to the competition's 100 m cells.

Physical signature: A coherent scarp cross-profile fit that allows finite initial and far-field slopes, co-located with ridge/valley responses persisting across neighboring physical scales and aligned along a trace. Template amplitude, morphologic age, orientation, and SNR must be computed by the cited template method rather than approximated by ratios or robust z-scores.

Why off-catalogue: An expert-labelled new fault may preserve a subtle surface scarp omitted from the public compilation. Native DEM evidence can preserve profile shape and continuity that are diluted by coarser aggregation. This is a geological rationale to test, not an observed gain.

Named non-fault mimic: Fluvial terrace riser. Also test natural channel banks, road/canal embankments, landslide headscarps, shoreline risers, and DEM seams.

Difference from repository: The present H66 code decodes owner-described 100 m stack proxies; it does not implement the actual Scarplet template fit or footprint-wide native Steger/Frangi scale sweep. Sibling owner reports describe LiDAR scarp channels and thinning, so the joined slope-aware template plus signed cross-scale line persistence is a candidate distinction, not a verified novelty claim. The full raster registry is unavailable.

Cost: Very high: tiled DEM/projection and edge handling, multi-orientation and multiscale operators, mask validation, aggregation, per-feature leakage tests, segment-blocked holdout/CI, full-registry scans, and shared-tool corrections. Runtime and storage are unknown until exact tiles are inventoried.

Expected gain: Highest qualitative prior in this ranking because it combines two complementary physical measurements; sign and size are unknown. Not a DTI projection.

Input availability: Blocked. Official competition rasters, tile list, shared cache, and existing cultural mask are absent. USGS 3DEP 1 m project DEMs are public domain in principle, but exact study-area coverage and compatibility are unverified (S04, S05, S07).

Validation: NOT RUN. This is the lead to review before implementation. Do not emit a candidate until authorized data, corrected shared holdout/writer, complete registry, independent scientific sign-offs, and budget are available.

Sources: S02, S04, S07, S09, S10, S11, S12

Rank 2 · LIDAR-02

Slope-aware scarp-template channels alone

Layers/features: The same authorized native DEM and shared cache; a template search over scarp orientation and morphologic age that estimates amplitude and template SNR while allowing nonzero regional slopes; aggregate amplitude, age, orientation, and SNR summaries to 100 m.

Physical signature: Consistent cross-profile curvature/step fit with a stable orientation and plausible along-trace amplitude despite a dipping surface and unequal adjacent slopes.

Why off-catalogue: Small or gently dipping scarps may be missed by regional mapping and weakened by coarse summaries while remaining present in bare-earth topography.

Named non-fault mimic: Road-cut or irrigation-canal bank; terrace risers and landslide scarps are additional controls.

Difference from repository: No actual template search is implemented in this checkout. The legacy fields named amplitude/SNR/relative age are proxies, not Scarplet outputs. Sare and Hilley establish a relevant published method, not novelty or competition benefit for this implementation. Registry-wide uniqueness remains unknown.

Cost: High: method-faithful template implementation, orientation/age calibration, tile boundaries, visible-fault-only feature construction, feature-alone canaries, blocked validation, and registry checks.

Expected gain: High qualitative prior for slope-tolerant scarp signatures; size and direction are unmeasured. No score projection.

Input availability: Blocked by the same missing authorized DEM tiles, cache, mask, and exact grid/reference files as LIDAR-01 (S04, S05, S07).

Validation: NOT RUN. Evaluate this as an ablation against a mass-matched baseline on the frozen spatial holdout before combining it with line channels.

Sources: S02, S04, S07, S09

Rank 3 · LIDAR-03

Signed, scale-persistent Steger ridge/valley and Frangi Hessian channels alone

Layers/features: The same native DEM and shared cache; scale-normalized Hessian structure, signed Steger ridge/valley responses, sub-pixel line locations, and orientation at 2–20 m; summarize response strength, sign, scale persistence, and orientation coherence at 100 m.

Physical signature: A paired, sign-consistent linear relief break persists at adjacent scales and traces a coherent line rather than appearing as an isolated edge at one scale.

Why off-catalogue: Native-resolution ridge/valley localization may preserve short or low-relief scarps that a single-scale or 100 m response misses.

Named non-fault mimic: Natural stream-bank line. Roads, field edges, terrace breaks, and DEM seams are additional controls.

Difference from repository: No footprint-wide Steger sub-pixel or Frangi scale sweep is present. These established line operators are not novel by themselves; owner-maintained sibling reports describe LiDAR thinning and scarp consensus. A complete registry is needed to test the exact signed/persistence surface for duplication or lane drift.

Cost: High: scale normalization, polarity/sign rules, edge suppression, tile seams, response calibration, feature-alone leakage tests, and full uniqueness/holdout gates.

Expected gain: Moderate qualitative prior with substantial non-fault mimic risk; sign and size are unknown. No DTI projection.

Input availability: Blocked by missing authorized DEM/cache; public availability of USGS 3DEP does not prove exact tile coverage or compatibility (S05, S07, S08).

Validation: NOT RUN. Test each signed response alone before any combination; apply the AUC leakage stop and same-fold mass-matched comparison.

Sources: S02, S04, S07, S10, S11, S12

Rank 4 · LIDAR-04

Ordered cross-profile polarity and along-strike continuity

Layers/features: The same native DEM and verified existing mask; sampled cross-profiles recording paired slope breaks, polarity, width, local relief, orientation, and along-strike persistence over 2–20 m; aggregate profile fractions and continuity to 100 m.

Physical signature: Repeated profiles with consistent step polarity and crest/toe ordering along a trace, rather than one isolated edge or a chain of unrelated line responses.

Why off-catalogue: A real fault scarp may be below regional-map inclusion criteria but retain repeated, similarly oriented cross-profile geometry along a trace.

Named non-fault mimic: Fluvial terrace edge; road/canal banks and landslide headscarps can also create ordered breaks.

Difference from repository: This is a proposed profile-order/continuity ablation inside the same LiDAR lane, not evidence of a separate lane. Existing owner reports describe scarp channels/consensus, and complete registry surfaces are not available; uniqueness is unverified.

Cost: Medium-high: profile extraction is tractable, but polarity handling, continuity thresholds, mimic controls, aggregation, ablation, holdout, and registry checks remain necessary.

Expected gain: Lower qualitative prior than the first three; may improve precision if ordered profiles separate faults from mimics, but may discard discontinuous real scarps. Unquantified.

Input availability: Blocked by missing authorized DEM/cache and existing mask; exact coverage/alignment is not established (S05, S07, S08).

Validation: NOT RUN. Prespecify profile/persistence thresholds, test each feature alone, then use the same segment-blocked holdout and uniqueness gates.

Sources: S02, S04, S07
