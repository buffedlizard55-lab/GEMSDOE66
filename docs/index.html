3 · Why 0.2778 was the family's best, and what actually beats it

The published winner h33-h33-2-b2 is 37,654 unit dots, none on the catalogue, the nearest one exactly 300 m away — the last rung of a pruning ladder in which every deletion of dots raised the score (0.1922 → 0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778). Under the official metric, DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w) with a 300 m triangular kernel, emitted mass is a cost: a dot pays only if its kernel credit beats 0.2·DTI (≈0.056 at 0.2778). So the score is bought by placement and near-minimal mass, not by a better probability surface.

Verified against the official public leaderboard today, 0.2778 is rank ~23, not the top: the leader is 0.4133 and 0.3195 is rank 9 — see the leaderboard and registry/leaderboard_snapshot_2026-10-11.json. Beating 0.3195 therefore needs more covered truth, not just tidier mass: which is why this lane was asked to bring an independent physical channel (1 m LiDAR scarps) and why its negative result matters — the LiDAR-scarp channel, however decoded, ranks off-catalogue structure only ~2× better than random dots, and every LiDAR-scarp emission this family has uploaded scored 0.043–0.159. The lever left is the geophysical/geo-energy convergence family, not scarps.
