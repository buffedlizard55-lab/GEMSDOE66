What this is

An auditable pipeline that produces one GeoTIFF of fault-probability predictions for the DOE GEMS Prize. The scored truth is faults that are not in the given catalogue, so every design choice here is aimed at that, not at recovering the 60,988 labelled pixels we were given.

The one idea that matters

The metric is a budget. With T = TPw, S = Σp, M = Σp·maxgk and |G| truth pixels:

DTI = T / ( 0.2·T + 0.2·(S − M) + 0.8·|G| )

adding a unit of mass raises DTI  ⟺  k > 0.2 · DTI

At DTI = 0.28 that bar is 0.056. Mass that cannot clear it is a loss. That is why the best-scoring rasters in this project family are sparse dot sets (~38 k pixels out of 5.17 M) and not dense probability maps.

The lane's skill is relief-dependent: strong on range fronts, moderate on mid-relief slopes, and absent on basin floors. That is the physics, not a bug — a tectonic scarp does not survive aggradation on an active basin floor, so there is no scarp for a scarp channel to see. It also means the lane's gain is not confined to range fronts (mid relief is not a range front), but it does vanish in basins, which is a limitation of this channel and is logged as one.
