## H33-2-B2: what the evidence does and does not say

The user supplied `0.2778` as the H33-2-B2 result. **Classification: user-supplied alleged score — unverified; neither `HOLDOUT-DTI` nor `ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED`.** No organizer submission-page receipt tied to the exact raster SHA-256 is in this checkout. The owner-maintained GEMSDOE32 page says no organizer score exists for its artifacts and describes its projected value as a model, not a score. The local audit sidecar also retains “UNSCORED | id see receipt.” It would be scientifically unsound to explain the cause of a score whose file-to-receipt association is missing.

A plausible mechanism — **not a finding about that alleged score** — is that the owner-described H33-2-B2 operation removed predictions near the already-mapped catalogue from a sparse prior. The official problem page specifies distance-weighted Tversky with α=0.2, β=0.8, and a 300 m triangular support (S02). Since β exceeds α, an uncovered true-fault cell carries the larger penalty, but unsupported prediction mass is still charged. Under the stated metric, for an isolated added unit of mass that improves credit to a previously uncovered truth cell, the marginal rule is `kernel_credit > α × DTI`; this explains why low-credit surplus pixels can hurt and why a selective pruning rule could help. It is not a way to infer H33's score, and no alleged value is substituted into it. If expert traces lie away from mapped catalogue traces, pruning may reduce low-value mass while preserving credit; if new faults run near known traces, it can instead remove useful credit. Spatial bias, emission budget, scoring round, or a different uploaded byte sequence are alternatives. Without a receipt and controlled, hash-pinned ablation, these explanations cannot be separated. See the local [metric derivation](../research/metric-derivation.md), which marks its distance conventions as assumptions rather than official wording.

The local H33 raster audit reproduces the file SHA-256 and basic binary-raster properties; that verifies local bytes, **not** a competition score. The owner page's stated baseline/pruning and user-provided leaderboard values are context claims only. A current top score of either supplied value cannot be authenticated from this checkout. The correct answer to “can we exceed the H33 figure?” is: **possible in principle, not demonstrated, and no reliable probability or expected score can be given**. A native-resolution scarp signal is a physically motivated experiment only if it improves the valid held-out metric, survives off-range-front analysis, and passes leakage and uniqueness gates. The old coarse proxy did not establish that.
