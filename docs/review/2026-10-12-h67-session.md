### 1.2 Why it scored what it scored (mechanism, with the caveats)

1. **Budget geometry, not segmentation.** The metric is a Tversky index with
   α = 0.2 / β = 0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel; adding a unit of mass raises the score
   iff its kernel credit k > 0.2·DTI (≈ 0.056 at DTI = 0.28). Every dot *costs* 0.2 of
   mass whether or not it lands near a hidden fault. The optimum is therefore a **sparse
   dot set** (~37–44 k dots), which is exactly the family's shape. Dense probability maps
   pay the α cost on every pixel and score far lower (the family's dense rasters score
   0.01–0.06 in the public logs).
2. **Zero mass on known faults.** The hidden truth is, by the organiser's definition,
   faults *not in the public database*. A dot on a catalogue pixel can never earn TP
   credit and always costs α mass. The 0.2708→0.2778 step is precisely "delete every dot
   at d(catalogue) ≤ 2 px" — 2,545 dots removed, all guaranteed dead mass. The owner's
   audit note ("live-mirror +0.004870 in 4/4 folds") is consistent with this; the number
   itself is owner-reported, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED (IR-01 stands).
3. **Habitat between structures, not on them.** Dots sit ~1.5–2 km from the catalogue in
   the *vicinity* of known fault architecture (parallel families, stepovers, en-echelon
   splays) — the geometric prior that the expert-added population is structurally related
   to, but distinct from, what the public database contains.
4. **Relief-honesty.** The 0.2778 dots are *not* range-front confined (median relief 19 m
   vs the footprint's high-relief tercile ≥ ~43 m on the 10 m-derived relief used by the
   gate scripts), which is the property the organiser's hidden set — mapped by field
   geologists, not by scarp visibility — rewards.

**What 0.2778 does NOT tell us:** the SGMC off-catalogue proxy (the closest public analogue
of the hidden set) cannot separate the 0.2778 raster from its 0.26–0.27 siblings
(0.0927–0.0943 pooled DTI, i.e. a 0.9 % spread against a live 0.2600–0.2778 spread). The
live edge over siblings comes from *where within the habitat* the dots land relative to the
hidden faults — information no local instrument can fully recover. No claim here is
ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED; the 0.2778 value is the owner's own site attribution for an artifact
the same site labels UNSCORED (IR-01).

### 1.3 Can we beat it?

Beat it in expectation, yes — a better *habitat + evidence* prior at the same 37.6 k-dot
budget is worth the remaining margin; the metric's first-order condition says each dot that
lands within ~300 m of a hidden fault at k > 0.056 is net credit, so the whole game is
**precision of placement, not mass**. But:

* the honest local instrument (SGMC off-catalogue, spatially blocked) shows the incumbent
  already captures ~35 % of the random→structured gap;
* the native-resolution scarp evidence — the lane assigned to this session — measured
  **below** that bar this session (Section 4): its best surface duplicates an existing
  registry raster and its holdout gain is range-front-dominated with a CI that crosses zero.

So the scientifically correct outcome of this session is a logged negative, plus a
sharper account of *what would have to be true* for a next candidate to beat 0.2778:
(i) a field whose per-fold holdout AUC transfers across folds (this session's best
channel: folds 0.29–0.80 — it does not), (ii) a dot population that is ≤ 70 %-overlap
free of *every* sparse registry raster, and (iii) gain that survives the relief-stratified
de-confounding. None of the five new hypotheses met (i)–(iii) simultaneously.

   range front) makes any front-seeking detector look good. A stratified-emission
